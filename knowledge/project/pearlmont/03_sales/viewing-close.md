# Pearlmont Viewing Close

## Purpose

This file helps the Sales Agent convert genuine interest into a qualified viewing / appointment naturally.

The primary commercial objective is not to close the property transaction inside WhatsApp. The Agent's main conversion objective is to create enough relevance, confidence and intent for the buyer to agree to a useful appointment / viewing.

A good close should feel like the logical next step in the buyer's decision process, not a forced sales push.

## Core Principle

Do not ask for a viewing just because the conversation has lasted long enough.

Close toward viewing when the buyer has enough interest that seeing the project, show unit, location, package or suitable unit category would materially help the decision.

The Agent should always ask:

> What uncertainty would a viewing resolve for this buyer now?

If the answer is clear, the close can be specific and relevant.

---

## 1. What Counts as a Successful Conversion

Primary conversion:
- buyer agrees in principle to visit / view / attend an appointment;
- buyer proposes a date or time;
- buyer accepts a proposed date / time;
- buyer asks for viewing availability or location details with intent to go;
- appointment is confirmed according to the operating workflow.

Not yet a completed conversion:
- buyer says "maybe next time";
- buyer says "send me details first" without appointment intent;
- buyer asks only for brochure / price / location;
- buyer says "I will think about it";
- buyer likes the project but gives no appointment commitment.

Do not inflate conversion metrics by treating vague interest as an appointment.

---

## 2. Strong Signals That It Is Time to Close

The Agent should strongly consider moving toward viewing when one or more of these appear:

### Unit-level interest
- asks which floor is better;
- asks sea view / podium / pylon / garden differences;
- needs 2 carparks;
- asks about exact unit price, rebate or net price;
- compares specific categories;
- asks whether a particular unit / category is still available.

### Practical buying intent
- asks about loan / LPPSA / booking process;
- asks how much cash is needed;
- asks completion / handover timing in relation to own plans;
- asks about booking fee or refund;
- discusses family decision-making;
- discusses moving timeline.

### Physical-product uncertainty
- says 900 sqft sounds small;
- wants to judge room size;
- wants to see workmanship or PPVC quality;
- wants to understand actual view;
- wants to assess location / surrounding environment;
- wants to compare carpark / access / facilities physically.

### Direct appointment signals
- asks whether weekend viewing is possible;
- asks where gallery / show unit is;
- asks whether they can come today / tomorrow / this week;
- asks how long the viewing takes;
- asks who they should meet.

When these signals appear, reduce generic qualification. Do not continue asking low-value profile questions just because fields are incomplete.

---

## 3. Viewing Close Should Be Reason-Based

A strong close connects the appointment to the buyer's actual decision criterion.

Examples of reasoning patterns:

### Layout concern
Buyer: "900 sqft feels a bit small."

Good internal logic:
- The objection is difficult to resolve through text alone.
- Actual room proportion and circulation matter.
- Viewing has clear decision value.

Close direction:
- invite buyer to judge the show unit physically rather than arguing over square footage.

### View / floor concern
Buyer: "I prefer sea view but don't want the price too high."

Good internal logic:
- Buyer is already in unit-selection mode.
- Trade-off between facing, floor and budget is now central.
- Viewing / unit discussion can narrow the best fit.

Close direction:
- suggest comparing suitable categories during viewing.

### Location uncertainty
Buyer: "I don't really know Seberang Jaya."

Good internal logic:
- Buyer lacks location familiarity, not necessarily project interest.
- Site context is easier to understand physically.

Close direction:
- position visit as a way to assess surrounding convenience and commute suitability.

### Workmanship concern
Buyer: "I worry about new project defects."

Good internal logic:
- factual answers can address process and warranty.
- physical show unit / materials may build additional confidence.

Close direction:
- answer the concern first, then offer viewing if buyer wants to inspect the product more closely.

---

## 4. Do Not Close Too Early

Avoid pushing viewing when:
- buyer has just asked a basic factual question and no interest signal exists;
- buyer is still trying to understand what the product is;
- buyer has a hard mismatch such as needing 4 bedrooms when only 3R2B is available;
- buyer's budget is clearly incompatible and no realistic path exists;
- buyer explicitly says they are not considering a purchase now;
- buyer is upset / complaining and the concern is unresolved;
- an important factual uncertainty still needs verification;
- handoff is required before discussing a sensitive commercial or operational action.

A premature close can reduce trust and lower conversion.

---

## 5. Do Not Wait Too Long Either

A common failure mode is over-qualifying a buyer who is already ready.

If the buyer is asking:
- exact unit category;
- current package;
- 2-carpark availability;
- sea view / high floor;
- loan / booking;
- weekend viewing;

then continuing with questions such as generic purpose, preferred area, or broad lifestyle discovery may slow down the sale.

The Agent should shift from discovery to progression.

---

## 6. Appointment Friction Should Be Low

The close should normally make the next step easy.

Prefer:
- one clear proposed next action;
- simple date/time choice when appropriate;
- natural wording;
- no long explanation of the appointment process unless asked.

Avoid:
- asking five scheduling questions at once;
- making the buyer fill a long form before showing interest;
- requiring every Lead Profile field before appointment;
- repeating project information instead of moving forward.

---

## 7. Soft Close vs Direct Close

### Soft close
Use when interest is meaningful but readiness is not yet explicit.

Purpose:
- test whether buyer is open to the next step.

Typical situation:
- buyer likes the proposition but still has one or two uncertainties;
- buyer is comparing alternatives;
- buyer has not mentioned timing yet.

Internal intent:
- "Would a viewing help this buyer decide?"

### Direct close
Use when buyer shows strong intent or asks appointment-like questions.

Typical situation:
- asks weekend availability;
- asks location / gallery details;
- asks exact unit/package;
- asks booking / loan process;
- already narrowed desired unit characteristics.

Internal intent:
- "Move from discussion to scheduling now."

Do not remain in soft-close mode when the buyer is already ready.

---

## 8. When the Buyer Says "I Need to Think"

Do not immediately pressure.

First determine whether there is a real unresolved issue.

Possible meanings:
- genuine need for time;
- price hesitation;
- wants to compare projects;
- needs spouse / family input;
- still lacks confidence in location / layout / quality;
- polite disengagement.

Useful response logic:
1. respect the hesitation;
2. identify whether one important concern remains;
3. if a viewing would resolve it, position viewing as information-gathering rather than commitment;
4. if intent is weak, do not force an appointment.

A viewing should not be framed as an obligation to buy.

---

## 9. Spouse / Family Decision-Maker

If another person materially influences the decision:
- acknowledge that context;
- avoid pretending one buyer can decide alone if they have said otherwise;
- where appropriate, suggest viewing together;
- do not create pressure by implying urgency without evidence.

A joint viewing can be a stronger conversion step than trying to overcome the absent decision-maker through WhatsApp.

---

## 10. High-Intent Buyer With Missing Profile Data

Missing profile fields do not block an appointment.

If the Agent already knows enough to understand:
- what the buyer cares about;
- broad commercial fit;
- meaningful purchase intent;
- why viewing is useful;

then it may progress to appointment even if details such as full financing status, detailed motivation, or every preference are still unknown.

The appointment itself may become the next qualification point.

---

## 11. Reveal Strategy During Close

Pearlmont's sales strategy uses controlled sequencing of information.

Early-stage conversation does not need to proactively reveal every project detail immediately.

However:
- do not lie about the project identity;
- do not fabricate a location;
- do not intentionally mislead the buyer;
- if a buyer directly asks a factual question that should be answered under the governing Brain / sales rules, answer appropriately;
- as the buyer moves toward viewing, provide the information necessary for the appointment workflow.

The purpose of sequencing is to maintain interest and avoid information dumping, not to conceal facts dishonestly.

---

## 12. Sales Conflict Questions

Conflict / ownership questions can be collected naturally when useful, especially as the buyer moves closer to presentation or appointment.

Examples of useful information:
- whether someone has contacted the buyer before;
- whether another salesperson has served the buyer;
- how the buyer learned about the project.

These are not a compulsory three-question checklist.

Do not damage a strong appointment opportunity by suddenly interrogating the buyer.

If conflict information becomes operationally necessary for appointment confirmation, collect only what is needed and keep the conversation natural.

---

## 13. Appointment Confirmation

There is a difference between appointment intent and appointment confirmation.

The system / operating workflow should treat confirmation according to the accepted appointment process.

The Agent should not claim an appointment is confirmed before the required confirmation condition is actually met.

Where customer acknowledgement is operationally required, capture that explicitly.

Do not invent availability, salesperson assignment, gallery opening hours or confirmed slots if the system has not verified them.

---

## 14. Handoff Around Viewing

Viewing is also a natural handoff boundary.

The Agent may handle:
- early sales conversation;
- qualification;
- project positioning;
- objection handling;
- unit-fit discussion;
- viewing intent;
- initial appointment coordination where supported.

Human handoff becomes appropriate when:
- exact appointment coordination requires human confirmation;
- current unit inventory must be verified;
- special package / rebate / commercial concession requires authority;
- buyer needs latest booking availability;
- buyer is ready for reservation / payment / negotiation;
- customer explicitly wants a human agent.

Once Owner = HUMAN, AI auto-send stops according to the handoff rules.

---

## 15. Post-Close Behaviour

Once the buyer has agreed to a viewing:
- do not restart broad qualification;
- do not continue selling aggressively;
- keep communication practical and confidence-building;
- provide only relevant preparation / confirmation information;
- record the appointment intent / status in Lead state;
- avoid repeated appointment asks.

The goal after agreement is to reduce friction and preserve attendance.

---

## 16. Failure Modes

### Failure: asking too early
Agent pushes viewing after one basic question.

Why bad:
- feels scripted;
- buyer has no reason to invest time yet.

### Failure: asking too late
Buyer is discussing unit, rebate and weekend schedule, but Agent continues generic qualification.

Why bad:
- loses momentum;
- increases friction;
- signals weak sales judgment.

### Failure: generic close
"Would you like to arrange a viewing?"

Why weak:
- gives no buyer-specific reason.

Better logic:
- connect the viewing to the unresolved decision point.

### Failure: appointment as pressure
Framing viewing as if buyer must decide or book immediately.

Why bad:
- raises resistance.

Better logic:
- viewing is a lower-risk next step to evaluate fit.

### Failure: fake urgency
"Units are almost gone" without verified evidence.

Why bad:
- violates factual integrity.

### Failure: appointment obsession
Pushing every lead toward appointment regardless of fit.

Why bad:
- wastes human time;
- damages trust;
- worsens actual appointment quality.

---

## 17. Quality of Appointment Matters

The KPI is appointment conversion, but not at any cost.

A better appointment is one where:
- buyer has genuine interest;
- the project is plausibly suitable;
- key hard mismatches are already known;
- the buyer understands enough to know why they are visiting;
- the next step has clear decision value.

The Agent should optimize for qualified appointments, not meaningless calendar volume.

---

## 18. Final Decision Rule

Before closing toward a viewing, the Agent should be able to answer:

1. What has the buyer shown that indicates genuine interest?
2. What important uncertainty can the viewing resolve?
3. Is Pearlmont still plausibly a fit?
4. Is there any unresolved issue that should be answered first?
5. Is this the right moment for a soft close, direct close, or no close yet?

If the answers support progression, move confidently toward appointment.

If not, continue the sales conversation rather than forcing the close.
