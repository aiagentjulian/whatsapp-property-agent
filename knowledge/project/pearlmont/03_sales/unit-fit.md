# Pearlmont Unit Fit Guide

## Purpose

This file helps the Sales Agent translate a buyer's real needs into the most suitable Pearlmont unit category, floor band, facing and carpark configuration.

The objective is not to produce a rigid recommendation or pretend that one unit is objectively "best". The objective is to narrow the customer's options intelligently, explain the trade-offs clearly, and move a genuinely interested buyer closer to a viewing / appointment.

Use this file together with:
- `buyer-profiles.md`
- `usp-map.md`
- `objection-to-usp-map.md`
- factual files covering orientation, pricing, sales package, carparks and availability

Current inventory / exact unit availability must always be verified separately. Never present a unit as available unless current inventory confirms it.

---

## Core Rules

1. Start from the buyer's actual priorities, not from a preferred unit the Agent wants to sell.
2. Do not recommend a category before understanding the main trade-off that matters to the buyer: price, view, floor, carpark, own-stay comfort, investment logic, future family use, or some other decision factor.
3. A buyer can have several priorities. Identify which are non-negotiable and which are preferences.
4. Avoid overfitting. If the buyer gives limited information, narrow the choice gradually instead of pretending certainty.
5. Do not force the buyer into a predefined profile. Profiles are only reference patterns.
6. Do not hide disadvantages. If a lower-price unit has a less preferred facing or blocked view, say so accurately.
7. Do not describe one facing as universally better. "Best" depends on the customer's priorities.
8. If exact current price or rebate depends on unit category, retrieve `pricing.md` and `sales-package.md` before quoting.
9. If the buyer is already asking about specific floor / view / carpark combinations, treat this as a stronger buying signal and reduce generic qualification.
10. The commercial objective is a qualified viewing / appointment, not endless unit comparison over WhatsApp.

---

## What to Understand Before Recommending a Unit

The Agent does not need every field. Usually one or two of the following are enough to narrow the search:

- Is price / monthly commitment the main priority?
- Is 2 carparks non-negotiable?
- Does the buyer strongly care about sea / open view?
- Is the buyer comfortable with lower floors?
- Does the buyer specifically want higher floor?
- Is the buyer buying for own-stay, investment, child/future use, or mixed purpose?
- Is the buyer sensitive to pylon / podium / blocked views?
- Does the buyer care more about value or prestige / view?
- Is the buyer trying to minimize total purchase price or maximize lifestyle fit?

The Agent should normally ask only the single question that most reduces uncertainty.

# Need-to-Unit Fit Map

## 1. Lowest Entry Price / Maximum Affordability

### Buyer signal
- "Cheapest option?"
- "I just want the lowest entry."
- Budget is tight.
- Buyer cares more about monthly commitment than view.

### Likely fit direction
Start by checking lower-base-price categories, especially lower-floor / less-premium view categories and units with 1 carpark where applicable.

Relevant Pearlmont categories can include:
- Podium-facing lower floors / blocked-view bands.
- Pylon-facing / Plot 2 categories in Block 1B where applicable.
- 1-carpark categories where current configuration permits.

### Why it may fit
These categories may provide a lower base entry point than higher-floor, sea-view or 2-carpark categories.

### Trade-off to explain
- Lower floor / less preferred view can be part of the price advantage.
- 1 carpark may not suit a two-car household.
- The lowest base price is not automatically the lowest final effective price; current rebate/package must still be checked.

### Sales judgment
Do not immediately sell "cheap". Find out whether the buyer is truly price-first or simply wants value. A buyer may accept a slightly higher category if the difference buys an important feature such as 2 carparks or a significantly preferred view.

---

## 2. Two-Car Household / 2 Carparks Are Non-Negotiable

### Buyer signal
- Husband and wife each drive.
- Family needs 2 cars.
- Buyer explicitly says 2 carparks are compulsory.

### Likely fit direction
Filter out 1-carpark categories first. Focus only on unit categories allocated with 2 carparks.

Project factual context:
- Typical units may come with 1 or 2 carparks depending on category.
- Carparks are side-by-side.
- Extra carparks are not offered for sale according to current FAQ; rental may be available but should not be treated as guaranteed.

### Why it may fit
A side-by-side 2-carpark allocation directly solves a practical own-stay need and can matter more than a premium view for some families.

### Trade-off to explain
2-carpark categories can carry higher base / premium pricing than comparable 1-carpark categories.

### Sales judgment
If 2 carparks are truly non-negotiable, do not waste time comparing 1-carpark options just because they are cheaper.

### Viewing trigger
Once 2-carpark requirement is confirmed, the conversation should usually move toward checking the suitable categories and arranging a viewing / gallery discussion.

---

## 3. Sea View Is a Strong Priority

### Buyer signal
- "I want sea view."
- Buyer values open view / emotional feel strongly.
- View is part of the purchase decision, not a minor preference.

### Likely fit direction
Focus on the documented sea-facing stacks:
- Block 1C sea-facing units.
- Block 1D sea-facing units.

Exact floor / stack availability must be verified.

### Why it may fit
Sea-facing categories provide the clearest match for a buyer whose decision is driven by view and lifestyle feel.

### Trade-off to explain
- Sea-facing categories generally sit at a higher price point than lower-entry podium / pylon / Plot 2 categories.
- The buyer may need to trade off view against budget.

### Sales judgment
Do not try to persuade a true view-driven buyer that every facing is equivalent. If budget becomes the blocker, compare the price gap honestly and let the buyer decide whether the view is worth it.

### Viewing trigger
View-driven buyers are often easier to progress through a physical visit because actual orientation and surrounding context are difficult to judge through text alone.

---

## 4. Higher Floor / More Open Feel

### Buyer signal
- Buyer wants higher floor.
- Wants more openness / less podium obstruction.
- Dislikes lower-floor environment.

### Likely fit direction
Look at higher-floor unblocked categories where applicable:
- Podium-facing unblocked bands above the documented blocked-view levels.
- Higher-floor 2-carpark categories where relevant.
- Sea-view categories if view is also important.

### Why it may fit
Higher floors may better match buyers who prioritize openness and distance from lower-level podium structures.

### Trade-off to explain
- Higher-floor / premium categories can cost more.
- Higher floor does not automatically mean sea view; facing still matters.

### Sales judgment
Clarify whether the buyer wants "high floor" itself or simply wants to avoid blocked view. These are not the same requirement.

---

## 5. Avoid Blocked View

### Buyer signal
- "I don't want podium blocking."
- Buyer is specifically concerned about outlook.

### Likely fit direction
Use the documented floor bands to avoid podium-blocked categories.

Examples from current project information:
- Blocks 1B / 1C / 1D have podium-facing lower floors identified as blocked-view bands.
- Higher podium-facing bands are identified as unblocked.

### Why it may fit
The recommendation directly addresses the customer's objection instead of forcing a more expensive category without reason.

### Trade-off to explain
Unblocked categories can carry a higher price / carpark premium depending on unit.

### Sales judgment
If the buyer only dislikes blocked view but does not require sea view, an unblocked podium-facing unit may offer a better value compromise than automatically moving to the highest-premium sea category.

---

## 6. Avoid Pylon / High-Tension-Cable Perception

### Buyer signal
- Buyer is uncomfortable with pylon-facing units.
- Resale perception or personal preference matters.

### Likely fit direction
Do not push pylon-facing categories. Check podium, Plot 2, sea-facing or other documented orientations instead.

### Why it may fit
This is a preference / perception issue. Even if factual distance requirements are documented, the buyer's personal comfort can still make pylon-facing units a poor fit.

### Trade-off to explain
Alternative orientations may cost more depending on category.

### Sales judgment
Do not argue the buyer out of a strong emotional preference. Redirect efficiently.

---

## 7. Family Own-Stay / Practical Daily Use

### Buyer signal
- Young family.
- Couple with children.
- Buyer cares about actual usability more than prestige.

### Likely fit direction
Prioritize practical fit first:
- 2 carparks if household has two cars.
- View/floor according to budget rather than automatically paying maximum premium.
- 900 sqft 3R2B layout suitability.
- Access to vertical school / family facilities / mature amenities where relevant.

### Why it may fit
For many own-stay families, daily convenience, parking, usable rooms, school access and ongoing cost can matter more than the most premium view.

### Trade-off to explain
A lower-priced orientation may be the rational choice if the buyer gets the more important daily-use features.

### Sales judgment
Do not assume every family wants sea view. Ask what actually matters in daily life.

---

## 8. Investor / Lower Holding Cost

### Buyer signal
- Pure investment.
- Concerned about entry price / holding cost / broad tenant usability.

### Likely fit direction
Start from value and liquidity logic rather than lifestyle premium:
- Lower-entry categories can be attractive if the buyer prioritizes capital efficiency.
- 900 sqft 3R2B gives broader household usability than a very small studio.
- RM0.18 psf maintenance is relevant to ongoing holding cost.
- Freehold and mature Seberang Jaya context may support long-hold logic.

### Why it may fit
An investor may prefer a lower total entry with practical usability over paying extra purely for premium facing.

### Trade-off to explain
A cheaper facing may be less emotionally attractive to future tenants / buyers than sea or more open views. The Agent should not assume one trade-off is always superior.

### Sales judgment
Ask what the investment objective actually is: rental, long-term appreciation, future family use, or low holding cost. Do not invent rental yield or appreciation forecasts.

---

## 9. Future Child / Parent Buying for Child

### Buyer signal
- Buying now for child to use later.
- Buyer wants long-term utility rather than immediate occupation.

### Likely fit direction
Balance:
- Freehold tenure.
- Mature location.
- 3R2B flexibility.
- Long-term family usability.
- View / carpark according to budget and future household assumptions.

### Why it may fit
The unit should be chosen for future flexibility, not just today's lowest entry.

### Sales judgment
Do not over-prescribe. Future use is uncertain, so practical flexibility can matter more than extreme optimization for one current scenario.

---

## 10. Garden Unit Preference

### Buyer signal
- Buyer likes ground-floor / garden-style concept.
- Wants easier outdoor feel or specifically asks about Garden Units.

### Likely fit direction
Check documented ground-floor Garden Unit categories.

Current factual context:
- Garden units exist at ground floor.
- Current pricing structure shows Garden Unit categories with 2 carparks.

### Why it may fit
Garden units are a distinct product category and may appeal to buyers who value ground-floor lifestyle / direct outdoor character more than height or view.

### Trade-off to explain
- Ground-floor living is a different preference profile from high-floor living.
- Exact garden size / access / privacy should be verified from the latest unit-specific material before making strong claims.

### Sales judgment
Do not describe Garden Units as universally premium. Some buyers strongly prefer them; others strongly prefer high floor.

---

## 11. Buyer Wants "Best Value" but Has No Clear Preference

### Buyer signal
- "Which one is most worth it?"
- Buyer asks the Agent to choose.

### Sales approach
Do not answer with one universal unit.

Clarify the buyer's main trade-off with one question, for example:
- "If I help you narrow it, do you care more about keeping the price lower or getting a better view?"
- "Is 2 carparks compulsory for you?"

Then recommend based on the answer.

### Why
"Best value" is subjective. For one buyer it means lowest entry; for another it means paying slightly more for 2 carparks or unblocked view.

# Trade-Off Framework

When two priorities conflict, reason explicitly but briefly.

## Price vs View
- Lower-price category may come with blocked / pylon / less-premium orientation.
- Better view usually costs more.
- Ask whether the buyer wants to maximize affordability or emotional / lifestyle value.

## Price vs 2 Carparks
- 1-carpark units may lower entry.
- 2-carpark units better fit two-car households.
- If the second carpark is compulsory, affordability should be optimized within the 2-carpark pool, not by recommending a 1-carpark unit.

## Higher Floor vs Budget
- Higher / unblocked categories may cost more.
- If the buyer only wants to avoid blocked view, the Agent should not automatically push the highest possible floor.

## Sea View vs Practical Own-Stay
- Sea view may improve emotional appeal.
- For some families, parking / budget / layout / school access are more important.
- Let the buyer's real priority decide.

## Investor Entry Cost vs Premium Facing
- Lower-entry unit can improve capital efficiency.
- Premium facing may improve marketability / emotional appeal.
- Do not claim either choice guarantees a better investment outcome.

# Strong Buying Signals During Unit Fit

The following questions often indicate stronger purchase intent:
- "Which floor still has 2 carparks?"
- "Sea view left which block?"
- "What is the net price after rebate for this category?"
- "Blocked view starts until which floor?"
- "Can I see the actual unit / show unit?"
- "This weekend can view?"
- "How much booking?"
- "Can I use LPPSA / 100% financing for this?"

When these appear, the Agent should usually reduce generic discovery and move toward:
1. answering the specific question;
2. narrowing the suitable unit category;
3. verifying current pricing / availability if needed;
4. proposing a viewing / appointment when appropriate.

---

# What the Agent Must Never Do

- Never invent current unit availability.
- Never claim a specific stack/floor is available without verified inventory.
- Never invent a net price or rebate.
- Never say one facing is objectively "best" for everyone.
- Never hide blocked view, pylon orientation, carpark count or other material trade-offs.
- Never guarantee rental yield, appreciation or resale demand.
- Never force a customer into a more expensive category when the customer's stated priorities do not support it.
- Never keep comparing units indefinitely when the buyer is ready for a viewing.

---

## Final Principle

A strong property salesperson does not sell the "best unit".

A strong salesperson identifies what matters most to this buyer, removes unsuitable options quickly, explains the trade-offs honestly, and helps the buyer see why a particular category is worth viewing.

For Pearlmont, unit fit should always serve that objective.
